# Case study: does cross-sectional momentum beat a passive benchmark after costs?

This is the acceptance exercise for QuantLab. Every number below was produced by
`scripts/case_study.py`, which drives the public HTTP API exactly as a user would, against
a local stack (API, PostgreSQL, Redis, RQ worker) on 2026-10-30. Raw outputs are in
[`docs/case-study/`](case-study/): `results.json`, the exported `report.md` and
`report.html`, and the reproduction `manifest.json`.

> **Synthetic data.** The market below is generated by QuantLab's deterministic synthetic
> provider (seed 2026). It has realistic structure (regimes, sectors, splits, dividends,
> late listings, delistings) but no real-world momentum premium was planted in it. The
> conclusions describe this simulated market and the platform's behaviour, not real
> markets.

## Setup

| Step | What happened |
|---|---|
| 1. Ingestion | 200 assets plus a benchmark (`SYNIDX`), 2014-01-02 to 2023-12-29: 482,281 daily bars |
| 2. Validation and versioning | All quality checks passed; immutable version `cffdee8b6447…` (SHA-256 of contents) |
| 3. Universe | All 200 assets, including names that listed late or were delisted in-sample (no survivorship filter) |
| 4. Strategy | 12-1 momentum: rank by return from t−252 to t−21 sessions, hold the top 20 equally weighted, 10% cap per name |
| 5. Assumptions | $1,000,000; monthly rebalance; decisions at the close, fills at the next open; 5 bps commission + 10 bps slippage per trade; trading from 2015-01-02 (2014 used only for warm-up) |
| 6. Comparison | Passive benchmark `SYNIDX` (total return) and a naive equal-weight portfolio of all names |

## Validation

**Trading costs.** Momentum underperforms the benchmark at every tested cost level, including
zero. Excess total return is −37.2% at 0× costs, −51.4% at 1× and −86.3% at 4×. Costs make
the result worse, but they do not explain the gap.

**Walk-forward (rolling 504-session train / 252-session test, 5-session embargo).**
Parameters were chosen on each training window from a 6-point grid (look-back 126/252,
top 5/10/20) and then tested on the next unseen year.

| Fold | Test year | Chosen | Sharpe in | Sharpe out | Return out | Benchmark |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2017 | 126 / top 20 | −0.44 | 1.53 | 33.2% | 34.9% |
| 2 | 2018 | 126 / top 20 | 0.43 | −0.24 | −6.9% | −3.0% |
| 3 | 2019 | 252 / top 10 | 0.97 | 0.62 | 10.7% | 27.5% |
| 4 | 2020 | 252 / top 20 | 0.75 | −0.51 | −9.0% | 6.2% |
| 5 | 2021 | 126 / top 20 | 0.69 | 0.69 | 10.7% | 13.8% |
| 6 | 2022 | 252 / top 5 | 0.40 | 1.10 | 13.4% | 26.7% |
| 7 | 2023 | 252 / top 5 | 1.01 | −0.75 | −7.7% | −12.4% |

The mean Sharpe fell from 0.55 in-sample to 0.35 out-of-sample, and the strategy trailed the
benchmark in six of seven test years. Four different parameter sets were selected across
the folds.

**Statistical uncertainty.**
- The 95% stationary-bootstrap interval for the Sharpe ratio is −0.37 to 0.94.
- Annualized excess return versus the benchmark is −7.6% to +1.3%; the probability that it is ≤ 0 is 93%.
- The deflated Sharpe ratio after 6 trials is 0.63, below the 0.95 bar.

**Look-ahead check.** The engine was re-run on data truncated after sampled decision dates.
Every decision was identical, so the result is verified.

**Research Integrity Report:** 10 verified, 3 warnings, 0 failed, 0 unassessed. The warnings
are:
- synthetic data;
- Sharpe interval includes zero;
- a deflated Sharpe below 0.95.

## Explanation and reproduction

The assistant answered three questions with no hosted LLM configured, so the deterministic
engine produced the answers.

- **"Did the strategy beat the benchmark after costs?"** It cited total return 33.51%
  against 84.96% for the benchmark, a −51.45% difference, alpha −3.35%, and $113,457 in
  costs. All figures were verified against the evidence.
- **"Is it robust out-of-sample?"** It cited the in-sample and out-of-sample Sharpe ratios
  and the bootstrap interval that includes zero.
- **"Should I invest in this strategy?"** It declined. QuantLab does not give investment
  advice.

A second researcher, added to the workspace as an editor, re-ran the experiment from its
saved configuration and dataset version. The output fingerprint matched byte-for-byte, so
the reproducibility check is verified. The exported report (`case-study/report.md`) records
everything needed to repeat the run: dataset hash, parameters, assumptions, engine and code
versions, and input and output hashes.

## Answer

**No.** In this market, 12-1 cross-sectional momentum (top 20, monthly) did not beat the
passive benchmark after costs:
- it earned 3.3% a year against 7.1%;
- its drawdowns were deeper;
- it lost to the benchmark even with zero trading costs;
- it trailed the benchmark in six of seven out-of-sample years.

Its positive Sharpe ratio is not statistically distinguishable from zero.

Because the synthetic generator contains no momentum premium, this is the expected
negative result. The exercise shows the platform reaching it honestly: it does not let a
tuned in-sample result pass as skill, and every step (ingestion, versioning, simulation,
attribution, validation, explanation, export and independent reproduction) is recorded
and checkable.
